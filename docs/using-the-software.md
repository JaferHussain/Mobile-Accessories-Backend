# Using the software — what to do first, and what comes after

**Moiz Mobile & Corporation, Danwran Lodhran**

A short guide to the order of work. Do the five setup stages once, in order. After that, only the
daily and monthly sections matter.

---

## Part 1 — Setting up, once (about half a day)

Each stage depends on the one before it. Doing them out of order means going back and redoing work.

### Stage 1 · Accounts — *Settings → Admin*

Create the two logins: **the owner (Admin)** and **the salesman (Staff)**.

The salesman never sees purchase cost or profit on any screen. That is why there are two accounts
and not one shared password.

### Stage 2 · Brands and Categories — *Sell → Brands, Sell → Categories*

Enter your **brands** (Vivo, Oppo, Samsung, Infinix…) and your **categories** (Mobile, Handsfree,
Chargers, Cables, Covers…). Either order — they are two independent lists.

> **Also create one brand for local goods** — call it whatever you like, for example
> **Localled Brand**. Items with no well-known maker (generic cables, covers, and so on) are filed
> under it. Every product must name a brand, and this is where those go.

Do both **before** products, because every product must name a category and a brand.

### Stage 3 · Suppliers — *Purchasing → Suppliers*

Enter each supplier you buy from, with their phone number.

Do this **before** purchases, because every purchase is recorded against a supplier. The software
then tracks what you owe each one.

### Stage 4 · Products — *Sell → Products*

Enter your items: name, category, brand, model, barcode, reorder level. Add a photograph if you
want one.

> **No prices here, and no quantity.** A product starts empty and unpriced — it is just the shelf
> label. Its **first purchase** sets the cost, the selling prices and how many you have, and that
> is what makes it ready to sell.

Both the category and the brand are chosen from the lists you set up in Stage 2, and both are
required. Generic stock goes under your local-goods brand.

**Enter the stock you have on hand today as the opening quantity.** From this point the software
counts stock for you — every sale reduces it, every purchase increases it.

### Stage 5 · Customers and old udhaar — *Customers*

Enter the customers who already owe you money from your paper register, and record what each one owes
as their **opening balance**.

> **Important — record an opening balance only once per customer.** If you enter it again, the
> software treats the new figure as a *correction*, not a second debt. This is on purpose, so a
> mistake can be fixed. But it means you should get it right from the register the first time.

You do not need to enter every customer now. New ones can be added during a sale.

## Part 2 — Every day

### Morning

**Enter the opening float** on *Money → Day close* — the cash you are starting the day with in the
drawer. It takes five seconds and it is what makes the evening count meaningful.

### Buying stock — *Purchasing → Purchases*

When goods arrive, record the purchase: supplier, product, quantity, **unit cost**, and the
**selling prices** — retail (what a walk-in pays) and wholesale (what a bulk buyer pays).

> **The first purchase of a product must set its retail price.** That is the moment it becomes
> sellable. On later deliveries the prices come up already filled in with what you charge today —
> leave them alone, or change them.

Stock goes up automatically. Two things to know:

- **The new cost replaces the old cost for all stock on hand** — not an average. If you bought 10 at
  800, sold 5, then bought 10 at 850, all 15 units are now valued at 850. This is deliberate: your
  profit is measured against what it costs you to replace the goods **today**.
- If you did not pay the supplier in full, the balance is tracked as **payable** until you do.

### Selling — *Sell → New sale*

1. Choose **Retail** or **Wholesale** at the top. The price changes to match.
2. **Scan the barcode** — the item goes straight into the cart.
   **Or type a name** — up to 8 matches appear, and you tap **Add** on the right one.
   *Nothing is ever added without you choosing it, even if only one item matched.*
3. Need something from another shelf? Go to **Products**, add it from there, and come back. **The
   cart is kept.**
4. Press **Proceed to sale** and answer two questions:
   - **Who is buying?** Walk-in · an existing customer · a new customer.
     *Always pick from the existing list if they have bought before* — otherwise the same person ends
     up with two records and you only see half of what they owe.
   - **How are they paying?** Cash · Bank transfer · JazzCash · EasyPaisa · Raast · Udhaar · Part
     payment.
5. Press **Complete sale**.

Then, from the receipt: **Print** it, **download the PDF**, or send it on **WhatsApp** or **SMS**.
Sending costs you nothing — it opens the message on your own phone and you press send.

For a bank or wallet payment you can also attach the **payment screenshot** to the sale, as proof if
it is ever disputed.

> **Udhaar and part payments can only be completed by the owner.** The salesman can sell for full
> payment and can collect money owed — but goods do not leave against a debt without you.

### Collecting udhaar — *Customers*

Open the customer, record the payment. Their balance falls and the receipt can be sent to them the
same way — it tells them what they paid and what is still remaining.

### Returns — *Customers & bills → Returns*

**Customer return:** find the sale by invoice number *or* by product name, enter the quantity coming
back, and the software works out the refund.

> If the item was sold at a discount, the refund is **what the customer actually paid**, and the
> screen shows all three figures — price, discount, refund — so you can explain it to them.

**Supplier return:** pick the supplier, pick the purchase, return the goods. Stock and what you owe
that supplier both adjust.

### Expenses — *Money → Expenses*

Record money going out, and **say where it came from — the till or the bank.** This matters: only
till money is counted against your drawer in the evening.

### Evening — Day close — *Money → Day close*

Count the cash in the drawer and type the figure. The software shows what the day *should* have left
you:

```
   opening float
 + cash sales
 + udhaar collected in cash
 − cash refunds on returns
 − expenses paid from the till
 − cash paid to suppliers
 ───────────────────────────
 = what should be in the drawer
```

If it differs, **write the reason in the note** ("Rs 300 to the delivery boy, not entered").

This is the only check the software has over physical cash. Everything else balances itself — a sale
can be recorded perfectly and the notes still go missing, and only this count would show it. One
short evening is an accident. The same short three evenings in a week is something to look into.

## Part 3 — Every week, and every month

### The Dashboard — *Dashboard*

Your overview for today, this month or this year: sales, purchases, profit, expenses, what is owed to
you, what you owe, stock value, and how much of your credit is full udhaar versus part paid.

### Reports — *Money → Reports*

- **Sales** and **Profit** — over any period
- **Profit by product** — what is actually worth stocking
- **Sales by salesman** — who took the money and who gave the discounts
- **Stock** — what is running low
- **Receivables** — everyone who owes you, ready for a phone call
- **Payables** — everyone you owe

### Backups — *Settings → Admin*

A backup is taken automatically every night and kept for 30 days. **Once a month, copy one to a USB
drive or your phone and keep it away from the shop.** A backup sitting on the same machine as the
software does not survive the thing you are protecting against.

---

## The six rules worth remembering

1. **Set up in order** — brands and categories, then suppliers, then products, then customers.
2. **Record an opening balance only once per customer.**
3. **Always pick an existing customer from the list** rather than typing them again.
4. **Say where every expense came from** — till or bank.
5. **Enter the opening float each morning and count the drawer each evening.** Without both, the
   count means nothing.
6. **Take a backup off the premises once a month.**

## If something goes wrong

- **"Only an Admin may complete a credit sale"** — a salesman is trying to let goods go on udhaar.
  This is working as intended; the owner must complete that sale.
- **"Not enough stock"** — the counted stock is lower than the system's. Correct it in *Products →
  adjust stock*, which keeps a record of the change and who made it.
- **A customer disputes a refund amount** — open *Returns → Recent returns*. Every return keeps the
  original price, the discount given, and the exact amount refunded.
- **An item has no well-known brand** — file it under your local-goods brand. Every product must
  name one.
- **"This product has no selling price yet"** — it has been added to the catalogue but never
  stocked. Record a purchase for it; that sets its price and puts it on the shelf.
- **Two records for one customer** — they were added twice during sales. Use the existing-customer
  picker from now on; the old balances can be moved across with an opening-balance correction.
